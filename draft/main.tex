\title{Spontaneous differentiation in collective learning}
\maketitle

\section{Literature}

Arola-Fernández and Lacasa described collective learning as local neural-network training coupled by diffusion of the model parameters \cite{arola2024}. Their effective theory is a disordered Ginzburg--Landau dynamics in which coupling competes with heterogeneity imposed by the private data of each learner. This heterogeneity is quenched: it affects local learning but does not evolve with the population.

We instead allow the source of heterogeneity to adapt. All learners are exposed to the same environment and have the same total learning budget, but a slow strategy redistributes that budget across the available samples. The strategy follows a fitness-driven replicator dynamics with weak symmetric exploration, while diffusion continues to exchange parameter information between learners \cite{page2002}. The resulting problem is whether a symmetric population can spontaneously differentiate into learners that specialize on different parts of a common environment.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Adaptive allocation in a common environment}

Learner $i$ has neural parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ and a slow adaptive strategy $\mathbf g_i$. All learners receive the same minibatch $B_t=\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{n}$ and use the same fixed representation $\boldsymbol{\phi}(x)$ of the environment. The strategy defines a sample preference
\begin{equation}
w_i(x_b\mid B_t)
=
n
\frac{
\exp\!\left[
\mathbf g_i^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol{\phi}(x_b)/T
\right]
}{
\sum_c
\exp\!\left[
\mathbf g_i^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol{\phi}(x_c)/T
\right]
},
\qquad
\frac{1}{n}\sum_b w_i(x_b\mid B_t)=1.
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The normalization fixes the gradient budget of every learner. The adaptive strategy changes where learning effort is spent, not how much total effort is available.

The neural dynamics is
\begin{align}
\theta_i^\alpha(t+1)
&=
\theta_i^\alpha(t)
-
\frac{\eta}{n}
\sum_b
w_i(x_b\mid B_t)
\partial_{\theta_i^\alpha}
\ell\!\left(f(x_b;\boldsymbol{\theta}_i),y_b\right)
\nonumber\\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta\sigma}{N}
\sum_jq_{ij}
\left(\theta_j^\alpha-\theta_i^\alpha\right).
\label{eq:microscopic}
\end{align}
For $T\to\infty$, $w_i\to1$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:microscopic} reduces to uniform learning in the common environment. Finite $T$ allows different strategies to emphasize different subsets of the same data stream.

To coarse grain this weighting, let $z_\mu(x)\geq0$, with $\sum_{\mu=1}^{K}z_\mu(x)=1$, denote a soft partition of the common feature space into $K$ learning directions. We define
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}
=
\left\langle
w_i(x)z_\mu(x)
\right\rangle_x,
\qquad
\sum_\mu a_{i\mu}=1.
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
Thus $a_{i\mu}$ is the fraction of learner $i$'s budget spent on direction $\mu$. If $\boldsymbol{\phi}(x)\simeq\mathbf e_\mu$ separates these directions and they occur with equal frequency,
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}
=
\frac{\exp(g_{i\mu}/T)}
{\sum_\rho\exp(g_{i\rho}/T)}.
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
The allocation $\mathbf a_i$ is therefore the coarse-grained effect of the adaptive strategy. Differentiation means that learners develop different allocations over the same environment, rather than receiving different environments.

\subsection{Adaptive effective theory}

The effective dynamics of Ref.~\cite{arola2024} is
\begin{equation}
\dot m_i
=
-m_i^{2D+1}
+
\delta_i m_i^D
-
\gamma m_i
+
\frac{\sigma}{N}
\sum_jq_{ij}(m_j-m_i),
\label{eq:arola-effective}
\end{equation}
where the local field $\delta_i$ represents heterogeneity in the private learning environment. In the present model the corresponding heterogeneity is generated by the allocation itself. To lowest order around the homogeneous state, we take the effective learning drive to be proportional to the allocated budget,
\begin{equation}
\delta_i
\longrightarrow
\delta_0 K a_{i\mu}(t).
\label{eq:adaptive-field}
\end{equation}
The factor $K$ makes $a_{i\mu}=1/K$ recover the homogeneous field $\delta_0$, while a frozen nonuniform allocation recovers a quenched heterogeneous field. Equation~\eqref{eq:adaptive-field} is the minimal linear closure connecting the microscopic budget allocation to the effective learning field.

For the first nonlinear case $D=1$, the fast dynamics becomes
\begin{equation}
\dot m_{i\mu}
=
-m_{i\mu}^{3}
+
\delta_0 K a_{i\mu}m_{i\mu}
-
\gamma m_{i\mu}
+
\frac{\sigma}{N}
\sum_jq_{ij}
\left(m_{j\mu}-m_{i\mu}\right).
\label{eq:fast}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:fast} retains the local Ginzburg--Landau dynamics and diffusive coupling of Ref.~\cite{arola2024}; only the field driving local learning has become dynamical.

Within the separated-direction closure, the softmax relation gives a simplex-preserving slow dynamics. We take the adaptive strategy to respond slowly to the component fitness according to $\dot g_{i\mu}=\varepsilon T F_{i\mu}$. Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmax} then gives the replicator term exactly. Adding weak symmetric exploration at rate $\nu$ yields
\begin{align}
\dot a_{i\mu}
&=
\varepsilon a_{i\mu}
\left(
F_{i\mu}
-
\sum_\rho a_{i\rho}F_{i\rho}
\right)
+
\varepsilon\nu
\left(
\frac{1}{K}-a_{i\mu}
\right),
\label{eq:slow}\\
F_{i\mu}
&=
\frac{1}{2}(1-\bar m_{-i,\mu})^2
-
\frac{1}{2}(1-\bar m_\mu)^2,
\qquad
\bar m_\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i m_{i\mu},
\label{eq:fitness}
\end{align}
with $\bar m_{-i,\mu}=(N-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j\mu}$. We choose $F_{i\mu}$ as the marginal collective contribution in direction $\mu$. An allocation is favored when it supplies competence that is poorly represented by the rest of the population.

\subsection{Spontaneous differentiation}

For $0<\delta_0-\gamma<1$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fast}--\eqref{eq:slow} have the positive symmetric state
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}^*=\frac{1}{K},
\qquad
m_{i\mu}^*=m^*=\sqrt{\delta_0-\gamma}.
\label{eq:symmetric}
\end{equation}
Consider a nonuniform eigenmode of the interaction Laplacian with eigenvalue $\lambda>0$ and a component perturbation whose sum over $\mu$ vanishes. Linearization gives
\begin{align}
\delta\dot m
&=
-
\left(
2m^{*2}
+
\frac{\sigma\lambda}{N}
\right)
\delta m
+
\delta_0 K m^*\,\delta a,
\label{eq:linear-m}\\
\delta\dot a
&=
\varepsilon
\left[
\frac{1-m^*}{K(N-1)}\,\delta m
-
\nu\,\delta a
\right].
\label{eq:linear-a}
\end{align}
The trace is negative. The symmetric state loses stability when the determinant changes sign,
\begin{equation}
\nu
<
\frac{
\delta_0m^*(1-m^*)
}{
(N-1)
\left(
2m^{*2}+\sigma\lambda/N
\right)
}.
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
The first nonuniform mode to destabilize is the Fiedler mode $\lambda_2$. For fixed mutation rate,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_c
=
\frac{N}{\lambda_2}
\left[
\frac{
\delta_0m^*(1-m^*)
}{
(N-1)\nu
}
-
2m^{*2}
\right].
\label{eq:sigmac}
\end{equation}
Diffusive coupling therefore stabilizes the homogeneous allocation, whereas weaker exploration allows differentiated learning strategies to persist. The timescale $\varepsilon$ changes the growth rate of the unstable mode but not its threshold.

We measure differentiation in the allocation and phenotype through
\begin{equation}
G
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\|\mathbf a_i-\overline{\mathbf a}\|^2,
\qquad
S
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\|\mathbf m_i-\overline{\mathbf m}\|^2.
\label{eq:orders}
\end{equation}
Both vanish in the symmetric state.

The nonlinear dynamics follows the mutation-dependent thresholds predicted by Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmac}. Below the threshold, learners separate into different allocations of the common environment and develop distinct phenotypes. Above it, diffusive coupling restores the symmetric population. The transition therefore arises from adaptive heterogeneity rather than from a prescribed partition of the learning problem.
