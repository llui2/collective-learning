\title{Spontaneous differentiation in collective learning}
\maketitle

\section{Literature}

Arola-Fernández and Lacasa modeled collective learning as local neural-network training coupled by diffusion of the model parameters \cite{arola2024}. Their effective theory is a disordered Ginzburg--Landau dynamics in which coupling competes with heterogeneity imposed by the private data of each learner. The resulting transition shows how information can propagate through a population of individually limited learners, but the source of heterogeneity is fixed.

We make this heterogeneity dynamical. All learners can receive the same data distribution, while a slow learning strategy changes how a finite learning budget is allocated. Selection and mutation then act on the learning strategy rather than on the neural parameters themselves \cite{page2002}. The model keeps the local-learning and diffusive-coupling structure of Ref.~\cite{arola2024}, but promotes heterogeneity from a fixed input to an evolving degree of freedom.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Adaptive heterogeneity}

Consider $N$ coupled neural networks with parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$. All learners receive the same minibatch $B_t=\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{n}$. Learner $i$ also carries a slow genotype $\mathbf g_i$ that assigns the normalized sample weight
\begin{equation}
w_i(x_b\mid B_t)
=
n
\frac{
\exp\!\left[
\mathbf g_i^{\mathsf T}\sg[\mathbf h_i(x_b)]/T
\right]
}{
\sum_c
\exp\!\left[
\mathbf g_i^{\mathsf T}\sg[\mathbf h_i(x_c)]/T
\right]
},
\qquad
\frac{1}{n}\sum_b w_i(x_b\mid B_t)=1.
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The neural dynamics is
\begin{align}
\theta_i^\alpha(t+1)
&=
\theta_i^\alpha(t)
-
\frac{\eta}{n}
\sum_b
w_i(x_b\mid B_t)
\partial_{\theta_i^\alpha}
\ell\!\left(
f(x_b;\boldsymbol{\theta}_i),y_b
\right)
\nonumber\\
&\quad
+
\frac{\eta\sigma}{N}
\sum_j q_{ij}
\left(
\theta_j^\alpha-\theta_i^\alpha
\right).
\label{eq:microscopic}
\end{align}
The first term creates local competence and the second only shares parameter information. For $T\to\infty$, $w_i\to1$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:microscopic} reduces to the uniform-learning form of the coupled dynamics in Ref.~\cite{arola2024}.

The slow strategy is selected by its contribution to collective performance. If $L$ is the validation loss of the full ensemble and $L_{-i}$ the loss without learner $i$, we use
\begin{equation}
F_i=L_{-i}-L.
\label{eq:fitness}
\end{equation}
Selection favors strategies with positive marginal contribution, while mutation prevents permanent fixation. The separation between neural learning and strategy evolution is controlled by a slow timescale $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Effective theory}

We coarse grain the genotype into an allocation $\mathbf a_i=(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{iK})$ with $a_{i\mu}\geq0$ and $\sum_\mu a_{i\mu}=1$. The variable $m_{i\mu}$ measures competence of learner $i$ along direction $\mu$. A minimal closure of Eq.~\eqref{eq:microscopic} is
\begin{align}
\dot m_{i\mu}
&=
r a_{i\mu}(1-m_{i\mu})
-
\gamma m_{i\mu}
+
\frac{\sigma}{N}
\sum_j q_{ij}
\left(
m_{j\mu}-m_{i\mu}
\right),
\label{eq:fast}
\\
\dot a_{i\mu}
&=
\varepsilon
a_{i\mu}
\left(
F_{i\mu}
-
\sum_\rho a_{i\rho}F_{i\rho}
\right)
+
\varepsilon\nu
\left(
\frac{1}{K}-a_{i\mu}
\right).
\label{eq:slow}
\end{align}
Here $r$ is the local learning rate, $\gamma$ the relaxation rate, and $\nu$ the mutation rate. We close the fitness through the same marginal-performance principle as Eq.~\eqref{eq:fitness},
\begin{equation}
F_{i\mu}
=
\frac{1}{2}
(1-\bar m_{-i,\mu})^2
-
\frac{1}{2}
(1-\bar m_\mu)^2,
\qquad
\bar m_\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i m_{i\mu}.
\label{eq:effective-fitness}
\end{equation}
Here $\bar m_{-i,\mu}=(N-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j\mu}$. The allocation therefore grows preferentially along directions for which learner $i$ supplies competence that is poorly represented by the rest of the population.

\subsection{Spontaneous differentiation}

The symmetric state is
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}^*=\frac{1}{K},
\qquad
m_{i\mu}^*=m^*=\frac{r}{r+\gamma K}.
\label{eq:symmetric}
\end{equation}
Let $\lambda>0$ be a nonuniform eigenvalue of the interaction Laplacian. Linearization around Eq.~\eqref{eq:symmetric} gives
\begin{align}
\delta\dot m
&=
-
\left(
\frac{r}{K}
+
\gamma
+
\frac{\sigma\lambda}{N}
\right)
\delta m
+
r(1-m^*)\,\delta a,
\\
\delta\dot a
&=
\varepsilon
\left[
\frac{1-m^*}{K(N-1)}\,\delta m
-
\nu\,\delta a
\right].
\end{align}
The trace is negative, so the symmetric state loses stability when the determinant changes sign,
\begin{equation}
\nu
<
\frac{
r(1-m^*)^2
}{
K(N-1)
\left(
\frac{r}{K}
+
\gamma
+
\frac{\sigma\lambda}{N}
\right)
}.
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
The first mode to destabilize is the Fiedler mode $\lambda_2$. For fixed mutation rate, differentiation is therefore possible below
\begin{equation}
\sigma_c
=
\frac{N}{\lambda_2}
\left[
\frac{
r(1-m^*)^2
}{
K(N-1)\nu
}
-
\left(
\frac{r}{K}
+
\gamma
\right)
\right].
\label{eq:sigmac}
\end{equation}
Stronger diffusive coupling stabilizes the homogeneous population, while weaker mutation favors differentiation. The timescale $\varepsilon$ controls the growth rate but not the threshold.

We measure differentiation directly through the dispersion of the slow and fast variables,
\begin{equation}
G
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\|\mathbf a_i-\overline{\mathbf a}\|^2,
\qquad
S
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\|\mathbf m_i-\overline{\mathbf m}\|^2.
\label{eq:orders}
\end{equation}
Both quantities vanish for identical learners and increase as the population separates into distinct learning strategies and phenotypes.

The nonlinear dynamics follows the analytical prediction. Below the threshold, learning allocations differentiate and produce distinct phenotypes; above it, diffusion restores the symmetric population. This transition is the new degree of freedom absent from the fixed-disorder theory of Ref.~\cite{arola2024}.
