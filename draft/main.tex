\title{Spontaneous differentiation in collective learning}
\maketitle

\section{Literature}

Arola-Fernández and Lacasa described collective learning as local neural-network training coupled by diffusion of the model parameters \cite{arola2024}. Their effective theory is a disordered Ginzburg--Landau dynamics in which coupling competes with heterogeneity imposed by the private data of each learner. The disorder is quenched: it changes the local learning dynamics but does not evolve with the state of the population.

We promote this heterogeneity to a slow dynamical variable. All learners can receive the same data distribution, while a heritable learning strategy changes how a finite gradient budget is allocated. Selection and mutation act on this strategy \cite{page2002}. The local learning law and diffusive coupling are inherited from Ref.~\cite{arola2024}; the new degree of freedom is the adaptive field that drives local learning.

\section{Results}

\subsection{From neural weighting to an adaptive field}

Learner $i$ has neural parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_i$ and a slow genotype $\mathbf g_i$. All learners receive the same minibatch $B_t=\{(x_b,y_b)\}_{b=1}^{n}$, but the genotype changes the relative contribution of its samples,
\begin{equation}
w_i(x_b\mid B_t)
=
n
\frac{
\exp\!\left[
\mathbf g_i^{\mathsf T}\sg[\mathbf h_i(x_b)]/T
\right]
}{
\sum_c
\exp\!\left[
\mathbf g_i^{\mathsf T}\sg[\mathbf h_i(x_c)]/T
\right]
},
\qquad
\frac{1}{n}\sum_b w_i(x_b\mid B_t)=1.
\label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The neural dynamics is
\begin{align}
\theta_i^\alpha(t+1)
&=
\theta_i^\alpha(t)
-
\frac{\eta}{n}
\sum_b
w_i(x_b\mid B_t)
\partial_{\theta_i^\alpha}
\ell\!\left(f(x_b;\boldsymbol{\theta}_i),y_b\right)
\nonumber\\
&\quad+
\frac{\eta\sigma}{N}
\sum_j q_{ij}
\left(\theta_j^\alpha-\theta_i^\alpha\right).
\label{eq:microscopic}
\end{align}
For $T\to\infty$, $w_i\to1$ and the genotype no longer affects learning.

To coarse grain the weighted gradient, let $z_\mu(x)\geq0$, with $\sum_{\mu=1}^{K}z_\mu(x)=1$, measure the contribution of sample $x$ to learning direction $\mu$. We define
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}
=
\left\langle
w_i(x)z_\mu(x)
\right\rangle_x,
\qquad
\sum_\mu a_{i\mu}=1.
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
Thus $a_{i\mu}$ is the fraction of learner $i$'s gradient budget assigned to direction $\mu$. If the representation separates the $K$ directions, $\mathbf h(x)\simeq\mathbf e_\mu$, and they occur with equal frequency, Eq.~\eqref{eq:allocation} reduces to
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}
=
\frac{\exp(g_{i\mu}/T)}
{\sum_\rho\exp(g_{i\rho}/T)}.
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
The allocation $\mathbf a_i$ is therefore the coarse-grained effect of the genotype, not an additional microscopic variable.

\subsection{Adaptive effective theory}

The effective dynamics of Ref.~\cite{arola2024} has the form
\begin{equation}
\dot m_i
=
-m_i^{2D+1}
+
\delta_i m_i^D
-
\gamma m_i
+
\frac{\sigma}{N}
\sum_jq_{ij}(m_j-m_i),
\label{eq:arola-effective}
\end{equation}
where the local field $\delta_i$ represents the effect of the private data. Equation~\eqref{eq:allocation} provides the corresponding field when heterogeneity is generated by adaptive learning. For $K$ coarse directions we replace
\begin{equation}
\delta_i
\longrightarrow
\delta_0 K a_{i\mu}(t).
\label{eq:adaptive-field}
\end{equation}
The factor $K$ makes the uniform allocation $a_{i\mu}=1/K$ recover the homogeneous field $\delta_0$. A fixed nonuniform allocation gives quenched heterogeneity; allowing $\mathbf a_i$ to evolve makes the field adaptive.

We use the first nonlinear case $D=1$. The fast effective dynamics is then
\begin{equation}
\dot m_{i\mu}
=
-m_{i\mu}^{3}
+
\delta_0 K a_{i\mu}m_{i\mu}
-
\gamma m_{i\mu}
+
\frac{\sigma}{N}
\sum_jq_{ij}
\left(m_{j\mu}-m_{i\mu}\right).
\label{eq:fast}
\end{equation}
This is the Arola local dynamics with an evolving rather than quenched learning field.

The softmax relation also fixes the form of the slow dynamics. If the genotype coordinates change according to $\dot g_{i\mu}=\varepsilon T F_{i\mu}$, differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmax} gives
\begin{equation}
\dot a_{i\mu}
=
\varepsilon a_{i\mu}
\left(
F_{i\mu}
-
\sum_\rho a_{i\rho}F_{i\rho}
\right).
\end{equation}
We add symmetric mutation at rate $\nu$ and measure fitness by marginal collective contribution,
\begin{align}
\dot a_{i\mu}
&=
\varepsilon a_{i\mu}
\left(
F_{i\mu}
-
\sum_\rho a_{i\rho}F_{i\rho}
\right)
+
\varepsilon\nu
\left(
\frac{1}{K}-a_{i\mu}
\right),
\label{eq:slow}
\\
F_{i\mu}
&=
\frac{1}{2}
(1-\bar m_{-i,\mu})^2
-
\frac{1}{2}
(1-\bar m_\mu)^2,
\qquad
\bar m_\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i m_{i\mu}.
\label{eq:fitness}
\end{align}
Here $\bar m_{-i,\mu}=(N-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\neq i}m_{j\mu}$. Strategies gain fitness by contributing to directions that are poorly represented by the rest of the population.

\subsection{Spontaneous differentiation}

For $\delta_0>\gamma$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fast}--\eqref{eq:slow} have the symmetric state
\begin{equation}
a_{i\mu}^*=\frac{1}{K},
\qquad
m_{i\mu}^*=m^*=\sqrt{\delta_0-\gamma}.
\label{eq:symmetric}
\end{equation}
Consider a nonuniform eigenmode of the interaction Laplacian with eigenvalue $\lambda>0$, together with a component perturbation whose sum over $\mu$ vanishes. Linearization gives
\begin{align}
\delta\dot m
&=
-
\left(
2m^{*2}
+
\frac{\sigma\lambda}{N}
\right)
\delta m
+
\delta_0 K m^*\,\delta a,
\label{eq:linear-m}
\\
\delta\dot a
&=
\varepsilon
\left[
\frac{1-m^*}{K(N-1)}\,\delta m
-
\nu\,\delta a
\right].
\label{eq:linear-a}
\end{align}
The trace is negative. The symmetric state loses stability when the determinant changes sign,
\begin{equation}
\nu
<
\frac{
\delta_0m^*(1-m^*)
}{
(N-1)
\left(
2m^{*2}+\sigma\lambda/N
\right)
}.
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
The first nonuniform mode to destabilize is the Fiedler mode $\lambda_2$. For fixed mutation rate,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_c
=
\frac{N}{\lambda_2}
\left[
\frac{
\delta_0m^*(1-m^*)
}{
(N-1)\nu
}
-
2m^{*2}
\right].
\label{eq:sigmac}
\end{equation}
Diffusive coupling therefore suppresses differentiation, while lower mutation allows differences in learning strategy to persist. The timescale $\varepsilon$ changes the rate at which the instability develops but not its threshold.

We measure differentiation by
\begin{equation}
G
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\|\mathbf a_i-\overline{\mathbf a}\|^2,
\qquad
S
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_i
\|\mathbf m_i-\overline{\mathbf m}\|^2.
\label{eq:orders}
\end{equation}
Both vanish in the symmetric state.

Numerically, the loss of differentiation follows the mutation-dependent thresholds predicted by Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmac}. The resulting transition has the same competition between local learning and diffusive coupling as Ref.~\cite{arola2024}, but the heterogeneity that drives it is generated and maintained by the learning dynamics itself.
